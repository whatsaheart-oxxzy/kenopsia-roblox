# Reference game "Machine Party" (Steam, appid 4108000) — full teardown: minigames, session flow, Machine persona, reception, virality, monetisation, and what a Roblox adaptation may/may not copy.

> Read-only research lens, measured 2026-08-22 (workflow wf_a13d7aec-54c). Facts cite their source; treat plan documents as claims, measurements as truth.

## Summary

Machine Party (appid 4108000, Mike Klubnika + GDeavid, pub. Oro Interactive, 30 Jul 2026, €7.49-7.99) is a 2-4 player collection of 15 lethal minigames — "Mario Party run through an industrial grinder". It sold 500k copies in week 1, 1M by 12 Aug, peaked at ~21,954 CCU, and sits at 88% / 1,488 positive of 1,694 reviews.
Crucially: there is NO spoken Machine narrator. The "judging Machine" persona is delivered ENTIRELY as green CRT text cards in clinical lab bureaucratese. Players are "subjects"/"test units"/"vessels"; minigames are "simulations"; the score is the "POTENTIAL GENE MUTATION SCORE". I recovered the exact strings from cached gameplay frames already in this repo (.tmp_watch_machineparty/) — see facts.
The session loop is a tight 4-beat cycle repeated 15x: (1) simulation roulette — a green CRT grid of 15 pictogram tiles that BLANK OUT as they are consumed; (2) briefing card "NEXT SIMULATION: <NAME>" with a CONTROLS panel (Page 1/2), live standings, INVITE slots and a READY button; (3) a black round card "Round N/M" + a 4-6 word uppercase imperative tagline ending in "._"; (4) an intermission scoreboard card headed "SIMULATION CONCLUDED. DISTRIBUTE NEURAL NETWORK MONITOR DATA POINTS". Most simulations are 5 rounds; Minefield is 10. Full run ≈ 1 hour; 100% ≈ 4.5 h.
Praise concentrates on: chaos-with-friends, the brutalist art style, per-minigame BESPOKE death animations (no generic ragdoll), the soundtrack, and the $8 price. Complaints concentrate on: ~1 hour of content, NO matchmaking/server browser (the dev explicitly refuses it — "friend-group focused"), fixed minigame order, stun-lock, desync, no practice mode, no progression.
Virality is creator-driven, not built-in: the dev thanked players for "cool clips"; it hit Twitch #65 in Aug 2026 at 4,934 avg CCU. The clip engine is the death moment itself — telegraphed hazard (red sniper laser, red/green alcove lights), instant gore, camera holds on the corpse, slow fade. Nothing about the game records or shares anything.
Monetisation is pure premium: one $8 purchase, no DLC, no battle pass, no currency; cosmetics are free jumpsuit-colour + headwear picked pre-lobby.
For the Kenopsia adaptation: docs/MP-04 §0.1 already bans all 12+3 reference minigame names and maps them to originals. The bans must extend to the CRT copy itself ("POTENTIAL GENE MUTATION SCORE", "SIMULATION CONCLUDED. DISTRIBUTE NEURAL NETWORK MONITOR DATA POINTS", "NEXT SIMULATION:", the numbered blue jumpsuit). What IS free to adopt is the grammar: roulette-that-depletes, briefing card with controls, round card with a terse order, intermission score card, bespoke deaths, and a text-only machine voice.

## Problems

### Copying the reference's fixed minigame order would import its single most-cited structural complaint

- **Evidence:** ~25 negative reviews cite 'games come in the same order every time'; the devs only added shuffle to CUSTOM playlists in v2.1.1 (13 Aug 2026), never to Original. Meanwhile the roulette UI VISUALLY promises randomness (a reticle sweeping a grid) that the Original mode does not deliver.
- **Impact:** On Roblox, where sessions repeat many times per day with rotating players, a fixed order is far more damaging than on Steam: returning players see the identical sequence every session and churn after run 2. The reference's own roulette theatre becomes a lie the player catches in one session.
- **Fix idea:** Make the roulette honest: seeded-random order by default, with the depleting-tile grid genuinely reflecting the remaining pool. Keep a deterministic 'first run' order only for a first-session tutorialised path, then shuffle.

### ~1 hour of total content is the #1 complaint and is fatal on Roblox retention metrics

- **Evidence:** ~45 negative reviews cite content volume ('you've played literally everything the game has to offer'), ~40 cite replayability; reviewers report 100% in 4.5 hours; the whole Original run is ≈1 hour.
- **Impact:** Roblox monetises and ranks on repeat sessions and D1/D7 retention, not on a one-off $8 purchase. A design that is 'complete' in one hour has no engine to bring players back on day 2. The reference could afford this because it took the money up front; a Roblox game cannot.
- **Fix idea:** Add per-trial variation axes the reference lacks (mutators, escalating difficulty tiers, seeded layouts) so the twelfth run of a trial is not the first run again, and add a between-session reason to return (rotating daily trial set, persistent cosmetic earn).

### No matchmaking / no lobby browser is a deliberate stance that does not transfer to Roblox

- **Evidence:** ~30 negative reviews demand matchmaking ('It NEEDS matchmaking, if no friends purchase the game...it's practically unplayable'); the devs explicitly reiterated in v1.5 that the game is 'friend-group focused'.
- **Impact:** Roblox's discovery model delivers strangers, not pre-formed friend groups. A design that assumes a full pre-arranged party of 4 and degrades badly below that will show an empty-server death spiral. The reviewer also notes 2-player matches feel 'quieter and less-unpredictable' — the design has a hard floor at 4.
- **Fix idea:** Design every trial to be legible and tense at 2, 3 and 4+ players with explicit per-count tuning (the repo already has Pacing.ROUNDS[id] = {[2],[3],[4]} — extend it upward), and lean on Roblox's native fill rather than requiring a friend party.

### No comeback mechanic plus permanent per-round elimination produces long dead time for losers

- **Evidence:** ~8 negative reviews cite the absence of progression/comeback ('winning just gives you points'); the round grammar is 'losers die on-screen' with 'no mid-round respawn and no score comeback'; deaths hold the camera on the corpse while survivors continue.
- **Impact:** A player eliminated in round 1 of 5 spectates four rounds. On Steam with a voice-chatting friend group that dead time is social; on Roblox with strangers it is an exit. The reference's own scoring is also flat (a single 'POTENTIAL GENE MUTATION SCORE' pool divided per simulation) with no rubber-banding.
- **Fix idea:** Keep the lethal, final death moment (it is the clip engine) but shorten the exposure: fewer rounds per trial, or give eliminated players a meaningful spectator verb (sabotage vote, hazard trigger) rather than pure watching.

### Host-authority networking produces the desync the reference is criticised for

- **Evidence:** The lifeisxbox review reports 'Shot James through a wall' and distance discrepancies attributed to 'desync during online play'; ~8 negative reviews report crashes/black screens mid-round.
- **Impact:** The reference's most-praised minigame (Duck Hunt) is precisely the one most sensitive to hit-registration: a telegraphed laser + instant headshot. If a Roblox adaptation of a sniper-vs-runners trial resolves hits client-side or trusts the shooter, the flagship trial becomes the most-complained-about one.
- **Fix idea:** Server-authoritative hit resolution for every lethal event; the laser telegraph should be a server-replicated warning, and the kill should be resolved from server positions, never from the shooter's client.

### Stun-lock and push-spam have no counterplay in the reference

- **Evidence:** ~8 negative reviews: 'you get stun locked' when pushed repeatedly and cannot defend yourself. Push is a first-class verb — the Stable Footing briefing card lists exactly two controls, 'Move' and 'Push'.
- **Impact:** Push-based sabotage is the reference's core social joy, but unbounded it lets one player deterministically delete another with no skill expression, which reads as griefing rather than betrayal. On Roblox this is amplified by strangers with no social cost.
- **Fix idea:** Give push a cooldown and give the victim a recovery window / stagger-immunity frames, so pushing stays a decision with timing rather than a spam button.

### There is no practice mode and no in-game rules surface beyond a briefing card

- **Evidence:** The lifeisxbox review flags 'Extremely simple on-screen instructions' with no practice rounds, explicitly contrasting with Mario Party. The briefing card carries controls on a 'Page 1/2' panel that appears for a few seconds before the round.
- **Impact:** On Roblox the median first-time player has zero context and often no voice chat. A 5-second controls card before a trial that kills you instantly means round 1 of every trial is a write-off for newcomers — which is exactly the round in which they decide whether to stay.
- **Fix idea:** Keep the briefing card as the aesthetic beat, but make its controls panel persistent (recallable during the round) and make round 1 of each trial non-lethal or forgiving on first exposure.

### The reference has zero built-in virality mechanism — copying it means copying nothing

- **Evidence:** Growth came externally: 'livestreams, creator videos, and social media clips'; the dev thanks players for 'cool clips'; the game itself contains no recording, replay, highlight or share feature. Contrast Content Warning, whose entire loop is filming your own deaths and uploading to in-fiction 'SpookTube' — it reached 204,439 CCU and 6.2M owners.
- **Impact:** A Roblox adaptation that copies the reference's structure will inherit its structural virality gap. The reference got away with it because a famous developer (Buckshot Roulette) plus a Triple-i showcase supplied the initial creator wave; a new Roblox title has no such carry.
- **Fix idea:** Build the share loop into the fiction the way Content Warning did — an in-world 'archive' or 'incident recording' that replays the death moment, gives it a shareable frame, and rewards the player for the most spectacular failure rather than only the win.

### Per-minigame bespoke death animations are the most-praised feature and the most expensive to reproduce

- **Evidence:** Reviewers single out that 'each death is unique, and it doesn't just use a generic blood splatter and die' — 'head blown off, cut into pieces, blown up, blown in half, fall to your death, turned into paste, spine snapped'. That is 15 distinct kill set-pieces.
- **Impact:** If a Roblox adaptation ships one generic ragdoll+red-particle death across all trials, it drops the exact element that reviewers name as the difference between this and cheaper party games — while still inheriting all the structural weaknesses.
- **Fix idea:** Budget death VFX per trial as a first-class deliverable, not polish. The repo's existing announce {kind='gorefx', pos, power} contract already parameterises this — vary the effect and the camera behaviour per trial, not just the power value.

### Roblox content policy makes a literal gore/smoking/execution copy unshippable

- **Evidence:** The reference's Steam mature-content notice reads 'Frequent gory outcomes from losing mini games'; the roster includes a competitive cigarette-smoking contest whose loser is executed by a trolley-mounted gun, and an execution-by-firearm dinner scene.
- **Impact:** Literal transposition of the gore level, the smoking minigame, and gun executions will fail Roblox moderation or force an age gate that collapses the addressable audience — while also being the thing that made the reference clip-worthy.
- **Fix idea:** Translate lethality into machine-language rather than blood: subjects are 'processed', 'voided', 'recycled' — hard cut, mechanical crunch, and a slot on the board going dark. The repo's §0.2 death-line rule ('PROCESSED.') already points this direction; the visual grammar should follow it.
